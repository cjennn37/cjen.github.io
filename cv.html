Pattern recognition has been the foundation of how I learn, understand people (including myself), and master my pursuits for over twenty years. Turning that lifelong instinct into a career was a natural evolution; each day, I look forward to using statistical techniques and algorithmic frameworks to reveal data insights that transcend human capabilities. I strongly believe that, by drawing parallels between data modeling and real-world experience, we can learn from the past to meaningfully enhance our future.

I am a recent M.Sc. graduate (2026) from the Department of Computing Science at the University of Alberta. I conducted my thesis research under the supervision of Dr. Adam White and Dr. Erin J. Talvitie, which focused on building robust selective planning algorithms for model-based reinforcement learning. Alongside my thesis, I have orchestrated several projects leveraging predictive models for computer vision, natural language processing, and bioinformatics. On the side, I regularly conduct quantitative analysis on large datasets tied to my hobbies. For a detailed list of my work, refer to my portfolio.

When I'm not analyzing data professionally, I'm usually applying the same analytical rigour to my passions, whether that's long-distance running, tennis, piano, or chess. Feel free to ask me how I track and model my own performance data for each of them, I'm always down to talk about stats!
